Standard SS-1 · Questions 1–8

Solutions & Solubility

Key Vocabulary (expect definition-style MC)

SolutionA homogeneous mixture of two or more substances uniformly distributed.
SoluteThe substance that gets dissolved (usually smaller amount). e.g. KCl in KCl(aq).
SolventThe substance that does the dissolving (usually larger amount). e.g. H₂O in KCl(aq).
UnsaturatedSolution that has NOT dissolved all the solute it can — more can still dissolve.
SaturatedSolvent has reached its maximum capacity for dissolving solute at that temperature.
SupersaturatedHolds more solute than normally possible — very unstable. Triggers recrystallization.
SolubleCan be dissolved.
InsolubleCannot be dissolved.
⚠ Exam Trap — Q1 & Q3 type In KCl(aq): the solute is KCl (what dissolves), the solvent is H₂O (what does the dissolving). The (aq) tag means dissolved in water — water is ALWAYS the solvent.
⚠ Exam Trap — Q2 type All solutions are homogeneous mixtures — uniform composition throughout. NOT heterogeneous.
⚠ Exam Trap — Q5 type MgCl₂(aq) is a solution because of the (aq) tag. Mg(s), Cl(g), MgCl₂(s) are pure substances, not solutions.

Calculations on the Exam (Q6–8 type)

% by Mass
% = (m_solute / m_solution) × 100
m_solution = m_solute + m_solvent
Molarity
M = mol / L
Convert g→mol using molar mass. Convert mL→L ÷1000.
Dilution
M₁V₁ = M₂V₂
Stock × volume used = final M × final volume
Q6 type: 33.0 g KNO₃ dissolved in water → 133 g total solution. % by mass?
% = (33 / 133) × 100
≈ 24.81% by mass
Q7 type: 80.0 g NaNO₃ dissolved → 200.0 mL total solution. Molarity?
Molar mass NaNO₃ = 23+14+3(16) = 85 g/mol mol = 80.0 / 85 = 0.941 mol M = 0.941 / 0.200 L
≈ 4.7 M
Q8 type: 12.0 M HCl stock → make 500.0 mL of 1.45 M. How much stock needed?
M₁V₁ = M₂V₂ (12.0)(V₁) = (1.45)(500) V₁ = 725 / 12
≈ 60.4 mL of stock solution
Standard RE-1 · Questions 9–18

Reaction Equilibrium

Core Concepts

Q (exam-style): When a system is at equilibrium, the forward reaction rate must be…
a. Less than the reverse reaction
b. Greater than the reverse reaction
d. Equal to the reverse reaction
At equilibrium, forward rate = reverse rate. Concentrations stay constant but reactions are still happening.
Q: Which symbol denotes a reversible reaction?
d. ⇄ (double arrow)
a. = (equals)
b. → (single arrow)
Single arrow → means one direction only. Double arrow ⇄ means the reaction goes both ways.
Q11: At equilibrium, concentrations of reactants and products will always be…
b. Equal
c. Changing
d. Constant
Constant ≠ equal. They stay steady but reactants and products can have very different concentrations.
Q12: All chemical equilibrium systems are described as…
a. Static
d. Dynamic
Dynamic = both reactions still occurring simultaneously. Static would mean nothing is moving — that's wrong.
Q13 (Le Chatelier): When equilibrium is disturbed, the system…
d. Temporarily loses equilibrium but finds a new equilibrium
b. Stays at the original equilibrium
Le Chatelier's Principle: the system shifts to OPPOSE the change and reach a NEW equilibrium.
Q14: A reaction at equilibrium — which is INCORRECT?
a. The reaction has completely stopped ← INCORRECT
b. Products are continuously being formed ✓
c. Rate forward = rate reverse ✓
d. Concentrations remain constant ✓
Equilibrium is DYNAMIC — reactions never stop, they just balance out.

Le Chatelier's Principle — Shift Rules

What changesSystem responseShift direction
Add reactantUses up extra reactant→ Forward (more products)
Remove reactantMakes more reactant← Reverse
Add productUses up extra product← Reverse
Remove productMakes more product→ Forward
Q17: X + YZ ⇄ Y + XZ. YZ is added. Which occurs?
d. The forward reaction rate increases
Adding a reactant (YZ) shifts the reaction forward → more products form → forward rate increases.
Q18: X + YZ ⇄ Y + XZ. Some X is removed. Which occurs?
a. The reverse reaction rate would increase
Removing reactant X → system shifts REVERSE to make more X → reverse rate increases.

Reading Equilibrium Graphs

Graph Questions (Q15–16 type) On a concentration vs. time graph: equilibrium is reached when the lines flatten out and become horizontal. The reaction that starts HIGH and ends LOW is the reactant. The one that starts LOW and rises is the product. If products level off HIGHER than reactants → products are favored.
Standard AB-1 · Questions 19–27

Acids & Bases

Arrhenius Definitions

Arrhenius AcidReleases H⁺ ions in water. (e.g. HCl → H⁺ + Cl⁻)
Arrhenius BaseReleases OH⁻ ions in water. (e.g. NaOH → Na⁺ + OH⁻)
Strong Acid/BaseNearly 100% ionize in solution. (HCl, HBr, HI, NaOH, KOH)
Weak Acid/BasePartially ionize — less than 100%. (HF, CH₃COOH/acetic acid)
Q19: According to Arrhenius theory, a substance that yields OH⁻ ions in solution is a…
c. Base

The pH Scale

ACID
7
BASE
0 7 14
pH < 7 → Acid (more H⁺) pH = 7 → Neutral (H⁺ = OH⁻) pH > 7 → Base (more OH⁻)
Q20 (Indicator): A basic substance tested with universal indicator would appear…
a. Blue (pH 8–10 range on the indicator chart)
Indicator color chart: Red=1–4, Orange=5–6, Green=6–7, Blue=8–10, Violet=11–14. Basic = pH > 7, so Blue or Violet.
Q21: Strong acid HCl at 0.1 M → pH 1.00. Weak acid HF at same concentration would have pH…
d. Greater than 1.00 (weak acids have less % ionization)
Weak acids don't fully ionize → fewer H⁺ ions → higher pH than an equal-concentration strong acid.

pH Calculation Formulas (Q22–25 type)

pH (for acids)
pH = -log[H⁺]
For strong acids: [H⁺] = molarity
pOH (for bases)
pOH = -log[OH⁻]
For strong bases: [OH⁻] = molarity
pH + pOH
pH + pOH = 14
Always at 25°C
Kw
[H⁺][OH⁻] = 1×10⁻¹⁴
Divide to find the other ion
Find [H⁺] from pH
[H⁺] = 10^(−pH)
Also: [OH⁻] = 10^(−pOH)
Q22 type: pH of HOCl at 0.50 M concentration?
HOCl is an acid → [H⁺] = 0.50 M pH = -log(0.50)
pH = 0.30
Q23 type: pH of 0.70 M KOH?
KOH is a base → [OH⁻] = 0.70 M pOH = -log(0.70) = 0.15 pH = 14 - 0.15
pH = 13.85
Q24 type: Find [H⁺] and [OH⁻] for HBr solution with pH = 1.35
[H⁺] = 10^(-1.35) = 0.04 M [OH⁻] = 1×10⁻¹⁴ / 0.04 = 2.5×10⁻¹³
[H⁺] = 0.04 M, pOH = 12.65
Q25 type: NaOH solution with pH 12.35 — find [OH⁻]
pOH = 14 - 12.35 = 1.65 [OH⁻] = 10^(-1.65)
[OH⁻] ≈ 0.022 M

Titrations (Q26–27)

NeutralizationAcid + Base → Water + Salt. Equal moles of H⁺ and OH⁻ react.
IndicatorChanges color at the endpoint to show the titration is complete.
Equivalence PointMoles of acid = Moles of base. Reaction is fully neutralized.
EndpointVisible color change of indicator — used to identify the equivalence point.
Q26 type: 22.3 mL of HCl reaches endpoint with 18.3 mL of 0.30 M NaOH. Find [HCl].
Step 1: mol NaOH = 0.30 M × 0.0183 L = 0.00549 mol Step 2: mol HCl = mol NaOH = 0.00549 mol (1:1) Step 3: M HCl = 0.00549 / 0.0223 L
≈ 0.246 M HCl
Q27 type (buret problem): Initial KOH = 3.35 mL, Final = 29.55 mL. Find vol used, then find [CH₃COOH] if KOH is 0.15 M and acetic acid sample was 35 mL.
Vol KOH used = 29.55 - 3.35 = 26.2 mL = 0.0262 L mol KOH = 0.15 × 0.0262 = 0.00393 mol M acetic = 0.00393 / 0.035 L
≈ 0.112 M CH₃COOH
⚠ Buret Reading Tip (Q27) Burets read from TOP to BOTTOM (0 is at top, 50 at bottom). Volume USED = Final reading − Initial reading. Always subtract to get the total volume added.

Don't Lose Easy Points

Exam Tips

Solute vs Solvent

In KCl(aq): KCl = solute, H₂O = solvent. (aq) always means water is the solvent.

Homogeneous

All solutions are homogeneous mixtures — uniform throughout. Never heterogeneous.

Constant ≠ Equal

At equilibrium, concentrations are CONSTANT — but reactants and products don't have to be equal amounts.

Dynamic, Not Static

Equilibrium is dynamic — reactions still occur in both directions, they just balance out.

Weak Acid pH

Weak acid at same concentration as strong acid → HIGHER pH (less H⁺ because less ionization).

Base pH Strategy

Never plug [OH⁻] into -log for pH directly. Get pOH first, then pH = 14 - pOH.

Buret Readings

0 is at the TOP of a buret. Volume used = Final − Initial reading.

mL → L

Divide by 1000. ALWAYS convert before using any molarity formula.

Le Chatelier Direction

Add something → system shifts AWAY from it. Remove something → system shifts TOWARD it.
